# Black Gram (Urad)

Black gram is a warm-season pulse crop, growing best at 25-35°C. It is fairly drought-tolerant once established but is sensitive to waterlogging and frost.

It performs well across a range of soils but prefers well-drained loams with a pH of 6.5-7.5, and can tolerate moderately poor soils better than many other pulses. Rainfall of 60-75cm during the growing season, or supplemental irrigation, is sufficient.

Sown either as a kharif (monsoon, June-July) or a short-duration summer crop, at a seed rate of about 20-25 kg/ha with row spacing of 30cm. Being a legume, it fixes atmospheric nitrogen and needs only a small starter dose of nitrogen plus phosphorus.

Matures in 70-90 days depending on variety. Typical yields are modest, around 0.6-1.0 tonnes/hectare, but it improves soil fertility for the following crop.
