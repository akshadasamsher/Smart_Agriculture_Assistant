# Jute

Jute is a warm, humid-climate fibre crop, growing best at 24-37°C with high humidity (70-90%). It requires a long, frost-free growing season and does not tolerate cold or drought.

It thrives in alluvial, well-drained but moisture-retentive loamy soils with a pH of 6.0-7.5, typically in river-basin regions. It needs high rainfall (150-250cm annually, well distributed) or reliable irrigation, and tolerates brief waterlogging better than most crops.

Sown at the start of the pre-monsoon/monsoon season, broadcast or in rows with a seed rate of 5-8 kg/ha for row sowing. It is a fast-growing crop and requires timely weeding in the first month.

Harvested for fibre at 100-120 days, at flowering to early fruiting stage, followed by retting (soaking stalks in water) to separate the fibre. Typical fibre yields are around 2-2.5 tonnes/hectare.
