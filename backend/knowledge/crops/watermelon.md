# Watermelon

Watermelon is a warm-season vine crop requiring high temperatures (24-32°C) and long, sunny days for good sugar development; it is sensitive to frost and cool weather, which slows growth and delays ripening.

It prefers well-drained sandy loam soils with a pH of 6.0-6.8. It needs consistent moisture during vegetative growth and fruit set, but reduced watering as fruit ripens improves sweetness; it does not tolerate waterlogging.

Sown after the last frost once soil has warmed, in hills or rows spaced 1.5-2.5m apart given its sprawling vines. It benefits from moderate nitrogen early on and increased potassium during fruiting for sweetness and quality.

Matures in 80-100 days from sowing. Typical yields range from 25-40 tonnes/hectare under good management with adequate pollination and irrigation.
