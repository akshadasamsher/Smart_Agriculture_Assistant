# Moth Bean

Moth bean is an extremely drought-hardy, warm-season pulse suited to arid and semi-arid regions, growing best at 24-32°C. It is one of the most drought-tolerant pulses grown, able to survive with minimal moisture.

It performs well even on poor, sandy soils with low fertility, preferring a pH of 6.5-7.5, and is notably tolerant of drought stress though it does not tolerate waterlogging. It can be grown successfully with as little as 40-50cm of rainfall during the season.

Sown at the start of the monsoon as a rainfed crop, broadcast or in rows spaced 30cm apart, with a low seed rate (8-12 kg/ha). As a legume, it needs little external nitrogen and thrives with minimal input, making it a good choice for marginal land.

Matures quickly, in 65-90 days. Yields are modest, typically 0.3-0.6 tonnes/hectare, reflecting its role as a low-input crop for harsh conditions rather than a high-yield one.
