# Apple

Apple is a temperate-climate fruit tree requiring cold winters for dormancy (chilling hours of 800-1200+ hours below 7°C) followed by mild, dry summers. It grows best where temperatures range 15-24°C during the growing season, and struggles in hot, humid tropical lowlands.

Well-drained loamy soil with a pH of 5.5-6.5 is ideal; apple trees are sensitive to waterlogging and heavy clay. Moderate, evenly distributed rainfall (100-125cm annually) or irrigation is needed, especially during fruit development.

Trees are typically planted as grafted saplings during the dormant season (late winter), spaced 3-6m apart depending on rootstock. They take 4-5 years to bear a commercial crop and can remain productive for 20-30+ years. Regular pruning and thinning improve fruit size and quality.

Balanced NPK with an emphasis on potassium during fruit development supports better size and sugar content. Typical yields range from 15-30 tonnes/hectare in mature, well-managed orchards.
