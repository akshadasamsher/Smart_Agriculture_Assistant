# Cherry - Powdery Mildew

Powdery mildew of cherry is caused by the fungus Podosphaera clandestina. White, powdery fungal growth appears on the surface of young leaves, shoots and sometimes fruit. Leaves may curl, twist, turn purplish and stay small, and infected fruit can be marked or stunted.

Unlike many leaf diseases, powdery mildew does not need wet leaves; it spreads in warm days with cool nights and humid air, especially on young, tender growth in shaded, crowded canopies. The fungus overwinters in buds.

Prune to open the canopy and improve sunlight and airflow, remove infected shoots, and avoid excessive nitrogen that pushes soft new growth. Water at the base of the tree.

Protective sprays are most effective when started early, around the time young leaves emerge. Chemical treatment, if needed, should follow the product label and local regulations; confirm the right product, timing and dose with a local agricultural extension office or plant-protection officer.
