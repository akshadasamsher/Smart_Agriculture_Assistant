# Grape - Leaf Blight

Grape leaf blight, often called Isariopsis leaf spot (Pseudocercospora vitis), shows as irregular, dark brown to black spots on leaves, sometimes with a yellow halo. Spots may merge, causing leaves to dry up and drop early, which weakens the vine and can reduce fruit quality.

The fungus survives on fallen infected leaves and spreads by wind and rain. It is more common in warm, humid, wet weather, and late in the season on older leaves.

Remove and destroy fallen leaves, prune for airflow, control weeds, and avoid wetting the foliage. Keep vines well fed and watered to avoid stress.

Disease usually builds in the second half of the season. If it appears early or spreads fast, ask local extension about protection options. Chemical treatment, if needed, should follow the product label and local regulations; confirm the right product, timing and dose with a local agricultural extension office or plant-protection officer.
