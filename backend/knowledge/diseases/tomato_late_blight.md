# Tomato - Late Blight

Late blight of tomato is caused by Phytophthora infestans. Leaves show large, greasy, grey-green to brown patches, with a white fuzzy growth on the underside in humid weather. Stems turn dark, fruit develops firm brown, greasy rot, and plants can collapse rapidly.

It spreads quickly by wind and rain in cool, wet or very humid weather, and can come from infected potatoes or volunteer plants nearby.

Act quickly. Remove and destroy infected plants (do not compost them), avoid overhead watering, space plants for airflow, and remove volunteer tomatoes and potatoes. Do not save seed from infected plants.

Late blight can destroy a crop within days in wet weather. Contact local extension quickly for protection advice. Chemical treatment, if needed, should follow the product label and local regulations; confirm the right product, timing and dose with a local agricultural extension office or plant-protection officer.
