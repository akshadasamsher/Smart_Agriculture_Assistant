# Corn (Maize) - Northern Leaf Blight

Northern leaf blight is caused by the fungus Exserohilum turcicum. It produces long, cigar-shaped, grey-green to tan lesions, often 2.5 to 15 cm long, on the leaves. Lesions may merge and blight whole leaves, and in heavy cases reduce yield.

The fungus survives in infected corn residue. Spores spread by wind and rain. Moderate temperatures, heavy dew, frequent rain and humid weather favour the disease.

Use resistant hybrids, rotate crops, and bury or manage crop residue. Avoid planting maize after maize in fields with a history of the disease.

Early infection before or around tasseling is the most damaging. If it is spreading quickly, discuss fungicide options with extension. Chemical treatment, if needed, should follow the product label and local regulations; confirm the right product, timing and dose with a local agricultural extension office or plant-protection officer.
