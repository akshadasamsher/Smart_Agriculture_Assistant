# Tomato - Early Blight

Early blight of tomato is caused by the fungus Alternaria solani. Older, lower leaves develop dark brown spots with concentric rings in a target pattern and a yellow border. Leaves yellow and fall, and stems and fruit can show dark, sunken lesions near the stem end.

The fungus survives in plant debris and soil and spreads by wind and rain splash. Warm weather with alternating wet and dry periods, and plants stressed by poor nutrition or heavy fruit load, increase the risk.

Remove infected lower leaves, mulch to prevent soil splash, stake plants for airflow, rotate crops for two to three years, and water at the base. Keep plants well fed and watered.

Protective sprays may be used if the disease is spreading. Chemical treatment, if needed, should follow the product label and local regulations; confirm the right product, timing and dose with a local agricultural extension office or plant-protection officer.
