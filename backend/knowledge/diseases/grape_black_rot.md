# Grape - Black Rot

Grape black rot is caused by the fungus Guignardia bidwellii. Leaves show small, brown, circular spots with a darker border and tiny black dots in the centre. On berries it starts as a pale spot, then the berry turns brown, shrivels and becomes a hard black mummy.

The fungus overwinters in mummified berries and infected canes. Spores spread in warm, wet weather and young tissue is most susceptible. Humid conditions and poor airflow increase the risk.

Remove mummies and infected canes in the dormant season, train and prune vines for good airflow and sun, and control weeds. Avoid wetting the canopy with irrigation.

Protection is usually timed from early shoot growth through a few weeks after bloom, when berries are most vulnerable. Chemical treatment, if needed, should follow the product label and local regulations; confirm the right product, timing and dose with a local agricultural extension office or plant-protection officer.
