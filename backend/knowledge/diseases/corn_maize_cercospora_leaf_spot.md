# Corn (Maize) - Cercospora Leaf Spot

This is gray leaf spot of maize, caused by the fungus Cercospora zeae-maydis. Leaves develop narrow, rectangular, tan to grey lesions that run parallel to the veins and are limited by them. Lesions can join together and kill large areas of leaf, reducing grain fill and yield.

The fungus survives in infected corn residue left on the field. Spores spread by wind and rain splash. High humidity, warm weather, dense plantings and continuous maize or no-till fields favour the disease.

Rotate with a non-host crop, bury or manage residue, plant resistant hybrids, and avoid very dense planting. Balanced fertilisation helps plants stay vigorous.

Disease on the leaves above the ear during grain fill matters most for yield. If the disease is spreading early, ask local extension about fungicide timing. Chemical treatment, if needed, should follow the product label and local regulations; confirm the right product, timing and dose with a local agricultural extension office or plant-protection officer.
