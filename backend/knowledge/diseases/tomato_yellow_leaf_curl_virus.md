# Tomato - Yellow Leaf Curl Virus

Tomato yellow leaf curl virus is spread by whiteflies. Leaves become small, curl upward, and turn yellow at the edges, and plants look stunted with shortened growth. Plants infected early set little fruit.

The virus is not spread by touch or seed in a normal way. It is carried by whiteflies (Bemisia tabaci) that feed on infected plants and move to healthy ones. Warm weather and weeds that host whiteflies increase risk.

There is no cure for infected plants, so remove and destroy them to reduce the spread. Control whiteflies with insect-proof nets in nurseries, yellow sticky traps, weed removal, and reflective mulch. Use resistant or tolerant varieties where available, and start with virus-free transplants.

Because it is a virus, fungicides do not work. Whitefly control and resistant varieties are the main tools; ask local extension about suitable options. Chemical treatment, if needed, should follow the product label and local regulations; confirm the right product, timing and dose with a local agricultural extension office or plant-protection officer.
