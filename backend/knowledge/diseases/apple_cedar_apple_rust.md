# Apple - Cedar Apple Rust

Cedar apple rust is a fungal disease (Gymnosporangium juniperi-virginianae). On apple leaves it shows as bright yellow-orange spots, often with a reddish border, that may develop small tube-like structures on the underside. Heavy infection causes early leaf drop and can mark fruit.

The fungus needs two hosts: apple and juniper (red cedar). On juniper it forms galls that produce orange gelatinous horns in wet spring weather, releasing spores that blow to nearby apple trees.

Remove nearby juniper or cedar hosts where practical, and prune out galls from them before spring. Choose resistant apple varieties when planting. Good airflow and removing badly spotted leaves help limit spread.

Protective fungicide sprays are timed to the period from bud development through the weeks after petal fall. Chemical treatment, if needed, should follow the product label and local regulations; confirm the right product, timing and dose with a local agricultural extension office or plant-protection officer.
