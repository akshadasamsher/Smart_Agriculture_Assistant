# Apple - Apple Scab

Apple scab is a fungal disease (Venturia inaequalis). On leaves it shows as olive-green to brown, velvety spots that later turn dark and scabby; leaves may yellow and drop early. Fruit can get dark, rough, corky scabs and may crack or stay small.

The fungus survives winter in fallen infected leaves. In spring, spores are released during wet weather and infect young leaves and fruit. Cool, wet, humid spring weather and long periods of leaf wetness favour the disease.

Rake and destroy or deeply bury fallen leaves in autumn, prune to open the canopy so leaves dry faster, and avoid overhead irrigation. Where available, choose scab-resistant varieties. Remove and discard badly infected shoots and fruit.

Fungicide protection is usually timed from bud break through the wet spring period, because the fungicide works best before infection. Chemical treatment, if needed, should follow the product label and local regulations; confirm the right product, timing and dose with a local agricultural extension office or plant-protection officer.
