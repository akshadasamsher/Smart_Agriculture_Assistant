# Corn (Maize) - Common Rust

Common rust of maize is caused by the fungus Puccinia sorghi. Small, round to elongated, brick-red or brown pustules appear on both leaf surfaces and release powdery rust-coloured spores. Heavily infected leaves may yellow and dry.

Spores are carried by wind, sometimes over long distances. Cool to moderate temperatures and humid weather with heavy dew favour infection. The disease is often more severe on young plants.

Plant resistant or tolerant hybrids, which is the most reliable control. Plant at the recommended time, and avoid very late sowing that exposes young plants to cool, wet weather.

Mild rust seldom needs treatment. If pustules are widespread before tasseling, ask local extension whether a fungicide is justified. Chemical treatment, if needed, should follow the product label and local regulations; confirm the right product, timing and dose with a local agricultural extension office or plant-protection officer.
