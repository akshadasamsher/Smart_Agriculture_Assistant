# Grape - Esca (Black Measles)

Esca, also called black measles, is a complex trunk disease caused by several wood-decay fungi. Leaves show yellow or reddish tiger-stripe patterns between the veins that later turn brown and dry, while berries may show small dark spots and can crack or shrivel. Severe cases cause sudden vine collapse.

The fungi enter through pruning wounds and live inside the wood, so symptoms on leaves are a sign of internal trunk damage. Older vines and stress from drought or heat make symptoms more severe, and they can vary from year to year.

There is no cure for infected wood. Prune in dry weather, make clean cuts, protect large wounds where possible, and disinfect tools. Remove and burn dead or dying vines, and cut out affected wood well below visible damage in the dormant season.

Because the problem is in the trunk, sprays do not fix it. Ask a local viticulture or extension expert about trunk-disease management for your area.
