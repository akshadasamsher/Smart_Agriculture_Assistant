# Apple - Black Rot

Black rot of apple is caused by the fungus Botryosphaeria obtusa. On leaves it causes small purple specks that enlarge into brown spots with a purple edge, often called frogeye leaf spot. It can also cause rotting fruit with dark concentric rings and cankers on branches.

The fungus survives in dead wood, cankers and mummified fruit left on the tree. Spores spread by rain splash and wind, and infection is more likely on stressed or wounded trees and in warm, wet weather.

Prune out dead wood and cankered limbs during dry weather, and remove mummified fruit from the tree and the ground. Keep trees well watered and fed to reduce stress, and avoid wounding bark and fruit.

Sanitation is the main control. If the disease is widespread, ask a local extension office about a suitable protection programme. Chemical treatment, if needed, should follow the product label and local regulations; confirm the right product, timing and dose with a local agricultural extension office or plant-protection officer.
