# Potato - Late Blight

Late blight is caused by the water mould Phytophthora infestans, the cause of the Irish potato famine. Leaves show pale green to dark brown water-soaked patches, often with a whitish fuzzy growth on the underside in humid weather. Stems can turn brown and plants can collapse within days. Tubers may develop firm brownish rot.

It spreads very fast by wind and rain in cool, wet or very humid weather. It can survive on infected tubers and volunteer plants.

Act quickly. Use certified seed, destroy volunteer plants and cull piles, and avoid overhead irrigation. If infection is found, remove and destroy affected plants and do not compost them. Delay harvest for a couple of weeks after killing the haulms to reduce tuber infection.

Late blight can destroy a crop quickly in wet weather, so contact a local extension office promptly about protective fungicide timing. Chemical treatment, if needed, should follow the product label and local regulations; confirm the right product, timing and dose with a local agricultural extension office or plant-protection officer.
